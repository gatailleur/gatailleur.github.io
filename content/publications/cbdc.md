Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) have moved to the center of the central banking agenda, yet little is known about what shapes the way central banks publicly justify them. Applying a transformer-based topic model to central bank speeches, we classify 8,082 CBDC mentions from 1,016 speeches by 83 central banks (2014--2023). We identify five framing categories: (i) monetary sovereignty, (ii) financial inclusion, (iii) monetary policy, (iv) payment systems, and (v) procedural accountability. We then relate their use to the institutional and macroeconomic environment of the central bank. We find that CBDC communication reflects durable structural and institutional characteristics rather than short-run cyclical pressures. Central bank independence is the strongest predictor of a procedural-accountability framing, through which a potentially mandate-expanding innovation is presented as bounded and technically controlled. Regulatory quality and income shape the monetary sovereignty framing in opposite directions, capturing the capacity to contain private digital money on one side and exposure to it on the other. Inflation and unemployment play no systematic role. Turning to mechanisms, we find that project maturity shifts communication toward payment-system rationales, but that institutions and maturity operate as parallel determinants rather than a mediation chain. The tone of communication follows the same logic, becoming more positive with independence and as projects advance. CBDC agenda-setting thus reflects stable institutional positioning rather than opportunistic reaction to economic pressures.

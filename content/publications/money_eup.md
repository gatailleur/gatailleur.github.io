This paper studies how ideology and economic conditions shape political preferences over digital money in the European Parliament. During the Ninth Legislature, parallel debates on cryptocurrency regulation and the ECB’s digital euro created a rare opportunity to observe the politicization of money and assess whether it generates political polarization. Drawing on 2,516 tweets from 222 MEPs, we combine sentiment analysis with an unsupervised text-scaling model to identify the ideological and economic determinants of support for private and public digital money, and to trace how these preferences are connected. We document growing polarization over the legislature, both across and within European political groups, alongside a mirror-image pattern: anti-elitism, right-wing economic positioning, and exposure to inflation increase support for cryptocurrencies, while the same drivers underpin opposition to the digital euro. The text-scaling analysis shows that discourse polarizes around privacy and fears of an expanding ECB mandate, which raises broader concerns about the acceptability of the digital euro.
